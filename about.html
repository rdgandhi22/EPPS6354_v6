M.S SDAR Projects

Methods of Data Collection and Production (Fall 2024)

  • Social Media & Influencer Impact on Fast Fashion

Description: This research project explores the intersection of consumer behavior and social media marketing within the fast fashion industry. By using a mixed methods approach, my team integrated web scraping via YouTube APIs and TikTok with a structured Qualitative survey to analyze how purchased content is influenced by influencer content. By processing social media metadata and developing an R-based data pipeline to harvest, we identified a significant 2024 surge in influencer “spree” and “haul” content. The study highlights Gen Z consumers have a huge increase of “identification of need”. Consumers phase in favoring impulse purchases driven by influencer authenticity and urgency displayed through the screen. The project’s technical scope involves two analysis of marketing effects and conscious consumer opinions. Through word frequency distributions (Word Clouds) and sentiment analysis of scraped off of TiktTok ad insights and YouTube descriptions, we mapped thematic clusters that define modern fast fashion in the trade-off between “cheap” pricing and “quality pricing.” Despite the challenges of navigating data sparsity and API rate limits, the findings underscore that the parasocial connection/high energy of influencers remain effective for brand expansion and are drivers of the digital age.

Research Design (Fall 2025)

  • Project A: Gamifying Transit: Raffle-Based Incentives for the MBTA

Description: This research proposal explores behavioral economics at the intersection of increasing public transportation ridership, as it is designed to do so within the Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority (MBTA) system. The study suggests implementing a mobile-app-based raffle system where commuters earn tickets for purchasing fares, which is grounded in Prospect Theory. By shifting the incentive structure from guaranteed small discounts (such as local business gift cards and monthly LinkPasses) to high reward lottery, the project aims to take risks for high-value gains and leverage human tendency, a strategy is proven successful in the INSINC program in Singapore for transit models. A crossover experimental design for the proposed methodology was utilized for the 2026-2027 schedule. The experimental and control groups are assigned to specific subway lines (Orange/Green vs. Blue/Red). By collecting revenue data and monthly ridership and comparing it against 2025 pre-test baselines, the study seeks to determine if gamification can provide a cost-effective solution to the MBTA’s budget deficits while reducing social and environmental costs of private vehicle reliance. This project demonstrates a high-level application of causal inference and experimental design.

  • Project B: Impact of Student Organizations on Campus Belonging

Description: This study investigates the correlation between psychological sense of belonging and student organization participation at the University of Texas at Dallas (UTD). By utilizing an adapted version of the Psychological Sense of School Membership (PSSM) survey, our team collected data from 49 graduate and undergraduate students to measure variables across four categories: teacher-student relationships, sense of acceptance, sense of attachment, and peer relationships. Using Google Sheets for heat maps and data processing for visual analysis, we were able to calculate Likert scores to compare non-participants with participants. Our findings led us to fail to reject the null hypothesis, as the average belonging score for non-participants was 41.15 and nearly identical for participants with a score of 41.31. However, when looking at the data, it revealed that undergraduate students who were in exactly two organizations reported a higher sense of belonging as opposed to those who were in fewer or more groups. The most consistent positive outcomes for campus attachment and teacher-student relations were observed when participation in these organizations was done specifically twice a month or daily. Overall, this project highlights “quality over quantity,” and student integration and campus involvement can play a pivotal role in student retention.

Information Management (Spring 2026)

  • Vector Verse Database Project

Description: Although we have millions of books available to us, it’s still quite difficult to find a book that actually resonates with our personal taste. We are often faced with the problem of the “paradox of choice”. This is a problem that I run into often as an avid reader, and I end up choosing books based on author, genre, or the hottest/trending ones occurring at the time. Additionally, recommendation tools that are available only rely on global averages and fail to capture the essence of an individual’s preferences. I decided to build a relational SQL foundation that would provide high-performance recommendations from this engine. I was able to turn my focus towards data accuracy, mathematical vectors used for cosine similarity, and its ability to be a predictive analyzer. The recommendation logic is built on collaborative filtering using the Cosine Similarity metric. In the approach for this metric, it identifies deep relationships between users and their historical rating activity, which consists of shared behavioral patterns and does not rely on metadata as much, such as publication dates or genres. The fundamental idea is derived from the fact that if two users agree on ratings/quality of books in the past, then the chances of them agreeing on others in the future are highly likely. Moving this from theory to a functional engine, all the relational data stored in the PostgreSQL reviews table is converted by the system into a matrix where every individual book is represented as a column, and every reader is represented as a row. Therefore, within this coordinate system, a user’s entire taste profile is a vector signature. In this multidimensional space, every person is represented with an arrow pointing in a specific direction, and the calculation used between the vectors is the cosine of the angle given. A final scan of titles and neighbors rated highly is performed by the system, and filters out any books that the active user has encountered. It then ranks the title by its average neighbor score, where the final result displays the top 5 picks of books personalized for a user.
